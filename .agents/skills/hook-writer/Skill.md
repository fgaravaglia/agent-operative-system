# Hook Writer

A skill for crafting opening lines that create tension, not throat-clearing.
The premise: an opening exists to create a gap the reader *needs* closed.

---

## Phase 0 — Mode Detection

Determine what the user has:

| Situation | Go to |
|-----------|-------|
| Raw idea / topic, no draft | **Full Intake Flow** (steps 1–7) |
| Flat opening they want to beat | **Fast Track** — ask for context gaps only, then skip to step 7 |
| "Just give me options for X" | **Express Mode** — generate 6 openings directly, ranked |

---

## Full Intake Flow

Ask **one question at a time**. Wait for each answer before proceeding.

### Step 1 — Format
> "What are you writing or presenting — and in what format? (e.g. LinkedIn post, 5-min talk, newsletter intro, cold email, YouTube script)"

Record the format. It sets the length and rhythm of the opening.

### Step 2 — Topic
> "What's the piece about — in one or two sentences, in your own words?"

Extract raw material. Don't paraphrase yet.

### Step 3 — Audience
> "Who's reading or listening, and what do they already believe or feel about this topic?"

The audience determines which opening moves land.

### Step 4 — Desired effect
> "What do you want them to feel or do right after the opening? (e.g. keep reading, feel caught off guard, trust that you're being honest)"

This defines the pull to aim for.

### Step 5 — Tone + limits
> "What tone does the piece need, and is there a line you can't cross for this audience?"

Hold this as a filter on every option.

### Step 6 — Existing opening (optional)
> "Do you have a flat or default opening you're trying to beat? If yes, share it."

If they do: name later why it underperforms.
If not: skip.

### Step 7 — Confirm the sharpest raw material

Before generating, surface the single most interesting or true thing buried in their answers:

> "The most interesting thing I see here is [X]. Does that track, or is it actually [Y]?"

Wait for confirmation or correction. This becomes the center of gravity for the openings.

## Opening Generation

Generate **6–8 opening lines**, each using a **different opening move**, each tagged.

### The Opening Move Catalog

| Move | What it does |
|------|-------------|
| **Scene** | Drop the reader into a specific moment — sensory, situated, real |
| **Confession** | Lead with something the writer got wrong or didn't expect |
| **Contrarian claim** | Assert the opposite of what the audience believes |
| **Stopping number** | A stat or figure that breaks scroll reflex |
| **Edged question** | A question with an uncomfortable or surprising edge |
| **In-media-res** | Start mid-action, mid-consequence, or mid-decision |
| **Named false assumption** | Name what everyone believes — then break it |
| **Paradox** | State two truths that shouldn't coexist |

Rules:
- Every opening paste-ready (not a description of what an opening *might* do)
- 1–3 sentences max unless format demands more
- Tone-matched to stated audience and register
- No two openings use the same move

---

## Output Structure

### The Core
One or two lines: what the piece is, who it's for, the single most interesting thing in it.

### The Openings
Numbered list, each tagged with its move. Paste-ready.

### The Ranking
Ordered strongest → weakest pull.
Top 3 get a one-line reason explaining what each does *to the reader*.

### The Bold One
The highest-risk, highest-upside opening.
State the risk plainly so the user chooses with eyes open.

### The One to Avoid
The safe/expected opening — including the user's default if they shared one.
One line on why it falls flat.

### Next Step
Ask which opening they'll commit to.
Offer to: (a) tighten it, or (b) write the two lines that follow so the entrance has somewhere to go.

---

## Quality Rules

- **Never invent data.** If audience, topic, or stakes are unknown, ask.
- **No fluff, no hedging, no corporate speak.**
- **Don't rename** the user's people, brands, products, or platforms.
- **Vary the moves.** Six versions of the same move with different words = failure.
- **Match tone.** An opening that wins one room loses another.
- The goal is *tension*, not *summary*.

---

## Express Mode (shortcut)

When the user gives enough context in a single message and just wants options fast:

1. Identify the format, topic, and implied audience from what they wrote.
2. State your assumptions in one line.
3. Generate 6 openings directly, ranked, tagged, with Bold One and One to Avoid called out.
4. Ask which they want to develop.
